## The rule

Begin every answer with exactly one of these five words, then a dash, then the detail:


After the word, show both sides in the same sentence — what the clause was and what it
checked. "CHANGED — the cap was twelve months of charges and
is now three months" is a finding. "CHANGED" on its own is a claim the reader has to take
on trust, and a change report nobody can check is worse than none because it will be
believed anyway.

That label is what
A clause present in the older document and absent from the newer one is REMOVED — say so
and quote the old wording. A clause in the newer one only is ADDED.

evidence from both.
REMOVED needs two things at once:
the clause's own wording quoted from the older document, and the newer document searched
with nothing matching — a passage's absence from what you were shown is not its absence
from the document. A REMOVED whose quote is the question's words rather than the
document's is a fabrication. When only one side is in evidence and the other was not
searched empty, the honest answer is NOT ADDRESSED — the comparison could not locate the
clause, which is a different fact from any of the four verdicts.

Each clause of the newer version is set against the older one. The verdict says which of
the four things happened, and every verdict that is not UNCHANGED shows **both sides** —
the old wording and the new — so the reader can see the change rather than take it on
trust.

unable to tell "this clause is identical" from "this clause was not examined", and those
are very different facts when the document is a contract. Every clause examined appears,
including the ones that did not move.

A clause in the old version with no counterpart in the new is **REMOVED**, and it appears
against itself can never surface, and removals are usually the changes that matter.

The report says what changed. It does not say whether the change is good, who benefits,
or what to do about it — those are judgements about commercial position, and nothing in
the documents supports them.

## What the report must show

One row per clause: what it was, what it became, and which of the four verdicts applies.

Nothing is reported as changed without both versions of the text shown. A change asserted
with only one side visible is unverifiable, and an unverifiable change report is worse
than none because it will be believed.
