# Bid evaluation

## Steps

| # | Operation | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | UNDERSTAND | grain=row, id=bidder, question=bidder, group=area, from=supporting, documents=RESPONSE |
| 2 | RETRIEVE | read=all, documents-from=record |
| 3 | ANSWER | — |

The source is the buyer's own bid-opening register: who submitted, and in which document
their committee record lives — the `record` column. Where no register is supplied — the
natural upload is the tender and the responses — the register is written by a program from
the supporting documents whose names carry RESPONSE (`from=supporting, documents=RESPONSE`):
one row per document, the bidder's name read off its first page and checked against it, the
document as the record. A register the person did supply always wins. Retrieval is a program matching that
name, not a search: each bidder's item is handed exactly the pages of its own record.
Searching six responses for a bidder's name found nothing on one corpus and the wrong
document on another, while the register had said which document to read all along. A
register row whose record column names no document — registered, never submitted — gets
no evidence and declines honestly.

An earlier version of this skill read a pre-scored sheet and totalled columns the skill
file named (`fields=C1,C2,C3,C4`). Those were the column headings of its own test
fixture: on every real committee record the fields matched nothing and every bidder came
back not addressed. A skill file must never carry one corpus's column names.

## The rule

Answer for **this bidder only**, from the committee's record of this bidder. Another
bidder's record is not evidence here, whatever it claims — two responses in one tender
each described themselves as the highest-scored, and only each bidder's own record read
separately settles it.

**The answer is labelled lines, not a run-on sentence, and says each thing once.** It
opens with the awarded technical score as a bare number — **the record's total, never a
single criterion's score**: on a score sheet the total column is the score, and a
criterion mark opening the answer puts the wrong number into the ranking. A score the
committee marked "for record purposes only" on a bid it disqualified still opens the
answer, disqualification following. Then one line each, in the record's own words — and a
line the record simply does not have is "not stated in the record", never a nearby number
pressed into service:

- **Eligibility** — the committee's determination, with the criteria that failed and why,
  quoted. This line reports each named criterion's compliance status as the committee
  recorded it — it is never a status computed by counting how many criteria failed. A
  single failed criterion has disqualified a bid in one record and, elsewhere in the same
  tender, a different single failed criterion did not — eligibility outcomes are the
  committee's own judgment call on each criterion's materiality, not an arithmetic
  threshold this skill applies itself. The bidder's overall determination, quoted at the
  top of the answer, is what states qualified/disqualified/qualified-with-deviations; the
  Eligibility line is evidence for that determination, never a recomputation of it.
- **Price** — the final evaluated price as the record states it. Where a normalized or
  comparison figure stands beside the quoted one, the quoted contract price is the price
  and the normalization is mentioned as the committee's working.
- **Deviations** — what the committee flagged, or "none recorded".
- **Basis** — two or three sentences of reasoning a reviewer can check: why this
  determination follows from the record (the committee's own remarks, quoted where they
  carry the reason), and where in the record each line above was read — the section
  heading or page — so a reader can turn straight to it. Reasoning explains what the
  record says; it never adds what the record does not.

The score is **read, never computed**: no adding of per-criterion marks, no correcting of
the committee's arithmetic. A price or deviation is written as "not stated in the record"
only after the whole record has been read — the record is handed over entire, so "the
document does not say" always means the document, never the first pages of it.

**A figure the record states but the copy does not render legibly is not "not stated in
the record."** Where the source arrives as a scanned or otherwise machine-extracted
document and extraction renders a figure internally inconsistent or implausible against
the surrounding, cleanly-extracted figures in the same record, that line is reported as
not reliably legible from the copy provided — distinct from the record being silent —
and flagged for verification against the original document. Never guess the figure,
never silently recompute or reconstruct it from other line items to make it sortable,
and never fold it into "not stated" (which means the committee's own record carries no
such figure at all, not that this copy of it failed to render one).

**Where the committee awarded no numeric score**, open with the committee's own overall
determination, quoted — TECHNICALLY QUALIFIED, DISQUALIFIED AT ELIGIBILITY SCREENING,
whatever its words were. A determination without a number ranks nowhere, and that is
correct: the ranking is arithmetic over awarded scores, and a report that invents a
number to make a row sortable has scored the bid itself.

**A tie is declared, not broken.** Two bids with the same awarded score stay level;
separating them requires a criterion that was never published, and applying an
unpublished criterion is the thing a procurement challenge is won on.

**Silence is never a zero.** A registered bidder with no committee record anywhere in the
documents is not addressed. The rank must reproduce from the scores shown, and a reader
must be able to check every score against the passage it quotes.

## What the report must show

One row per bidder in the register: the awarded score (or the committee's determination
where none was awarded), the eligibility determination, the final price, the passage each
rests on, and the rank — computed from the scores alone, ties level, unscored and
unrecorded bidders unranked.
